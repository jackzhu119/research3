## Word processing, figures, tables and supplements

Research Articles follow title page, structured abstract, keywords, optional abbreviation list, Introduction, Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion, Acknowledgments, References and legends. The official Research Article template also includes Conclusions. Sentence-case titles and unnumbered headings should be used consistently.

No explicit continuous-line-number, double-spacing, margin or main-font-size requirement was found in the complete instructions. The current Word template has no `w:lnNumType` element. Line numbers can be added to facilitate review, but must not be described as a verified journal mandate. Figure lettering must be at least 6 pt, preferably Arial or Helvetica. Multi-panel labels are uppercase A, B, C.

Each figure is uploaded separately as JPG, EPS, PNG or TIFF. The minimum is 300 dpi for pixel/halftone images and 600 dpi for combined line-and-pixel graphics at the intended size. Captions should be separate from the artwork, figures numbered consecutively and all cited. **Colour publication is free in both print and online editions.** Deterministic, editable scientific plots derived from the actual numeric results are appropriate.

Tables must remain editable Word or LaTeX tables, rather than screenshots or Excel files. Supply titles, column headings/units, explanatory alphabetical footnotes and expanded abbreviations. Equations should remain editable, rather than images.

The category table gives eight combined figures and tables for a Research Article. A separate section explicitly permits online Supplementary Material, but does **not explicitly state whether supplements are exempt from that eight-item count**. Keep the main article at or below eight and report the separately numbered supplementary items transparently. Each supplementary file must be **no larger than 10 MB** and publication ready; it will not be typeset. Cite supplementary figures/tables consistently. Do not treat an oversized all-in-one delivery ZIP as an acceptable individual supplementary submission file.

## Reference style

The journal uses Vancouver style, not MDPI/ACS. In-text citations use square brackets, for example [1], [2, 8, 25], [5–12]. The reference list is numbered consecutively without square brackets around its labels. Use the journal's examples with abbreviated journal names, year, volume and page/article number. For more than six authors, list the first six followed by et al. Every listed reference must be cited and every citation must resolve. Dataset records should include a persistent identifier where available.

## Ethical and legal declarations

The linked official DOCX contains these fields, in this order:

1. Acknowledgments.
2. Research ethics.
3. Informed consent.
4. Author contributions.
5. Use of Large Language Models, AI and Machine Learning Tools.
6. Conflict of interest.
7. Research funding.
8. Data availability.

Software availability and clinical-trial registration are among optional additional statements. The instructions and homepage require a customized template as a separate Word file. A self-authored equivalent may be useful for author review, but its acceptance as a replacement for the required template has not been established. A privately filled official template can be prepared for actual submission. Keep the declaration file and main manuscript consistent; the publisher says the template content is used in final typesetting.

The current official template has **no signature or signing-date fields**. Its date instruction concerns IRB approval where applicable. No handwritten signatures should be invented. In particular, the default author-contribution sentence includes responsibility and approval of submission; it cannot be asserted before the authors actually approve the manuscript.

The journal's standard no-conflict wording is **“The authors state no conflict of interest.”** Use it only when true and confirmed. Its no-funding default is **“None declared.”** This can reflect the user's confirmed absence of external funding; no grant should be fabricated. For research ethics and informed consent, explain secondary public-data analysis and the actual source-study provenance and applicable legal/institutional basis; never claim a local IRB granted an exemption when none was obtained. The template permits “Not applicable” where review or consent is not required, but public availability alone is not proof of that assessment.

The homepage identifies **Data Sharing Tier 2**, encouraging a data availability statement. The required declarations template nonetheless includes a Data availability field. Provide a full statement with the pinned analysis/results/code links and original source-data citations, rather than using the word “encouraged” to omit reproducibility information.

## Current AI policy

The journal-specific instructions (p.3) do not permit AI tools as authors. They require methodology/design use in Methods and manuscript-preparation use in the Ethical and Legal Declarations template. The separately retrieved publisher AI policy is marked **September 2026** and requires disclosure when AI output contributes substantially to the manuscript, while exempting simple proofreading/copyediting. It calls for disclosure in Acknowledgments. Keep Methods, Acknowledgments and the declaration form consistent about the actual work performed; this task includes substantive drafting and interpretation, not proofreading alone.

The publisher's authorship paragraph rejects AI-generated papers and places full accountability on human authors; its disclosure paragraph permits transparent assistance. Do not infer that disclosure alone certifies acceptability or human intellectual ownership. This remains an **author-review package**, pending the authors' substantive verification, revision, responsibility and final approval.

The current policy prohibits generative creation, alteration or manipulation of research data, figures and experimental images. It separately allows legally compliant illustrative AI images with disclosure. Here the quantitative plots should be generated deterministically from real archived data; no invented experimental imagery is appropriate.

## 2026 Subscribe to Open and charges

The current BMTE homepage states that its current volume is open access through Subscribe to Open, at no publication cost to authors. This is corroborated by the publisher's **April 2026 official announcement** that all 124 S2O titles met the annual threshold in 2026. Thus the 2026 zero-publication-fee statement is backed by current official sources, not merely by the user's screenshot.

S2O is assessed **annually**. The instructions explicitly say that when an annual threshold is not met, that year's content can remain subscription access, with green self-archiving potentially available under the publisher's policy. **Do not guarantee 2027 open-access terms or fees from the 2026 confirmation.** At an actual later submission/acceptance, verify the applicable publication-year terms.

No additional BMTE page charge or colour charge was found in the current instructions; colour in print and online is explicitly free. Optional professional language-editing services are at the authors' expense, and optional services must not be conflated with publication APCs. No fee was paid or payment promised during this task.

## Existing public drafts and submission declarations

The instructions exempt congress abstracts, academic theses and preprints on recognized servers such as arXiv, RePEc, bioRxiv and ResearchSquare from pre-publication. **GitHub is not explicitly identified as one of those recognized preprint servers.** The earlier public GitHub author-review draft must therefore be disclosed in the cover letter with its URL/date, its relationship to the present empirical manuscript and the shared data. Do not claim “never publicly disseminated,” and do not assume the recognized-server exception has already been granted. This preparation task does not establish an editorial ruling on the draft.

Retain accurate exclusive-submission and all-author approval declarations only when actually confirmed. Institutional email is strongly recommended by the journal, but is not expressed as a hard requirement; preserve the author's supplied Gmail and do not invent an institutional address. The corresponding author's complete street address and postcode should be verified because the journal asks for them explicitly.
